Is that a person moving the mouse?

A frozen Random Forest (AUGMENTED-18 / AUG_NAIVE_PLUS) scores your movement as you go. The browser computes the 18 features, bit-for-bit identical to the Java pipeline (verified by demo/parity.js), and sends only those numbers to the local scorer. Movement is split at 3 s gaps, and a chunk needs at least 20 distinct-time samples.

Read before demoing. The model was trained where human = Balabit 40–80 min admin-work sessions and bot = synthetic twins of those. This demo page is a third domain it has never seen, and absolute-velocity features depend on your screen, mouse and DPI — so treat the raw P(bot) score and the gauge as the signal, not the hard label. The 0.87 decision line is the Tier 5 operating point for a 1% false-positive budget on held-out users; at that setting the model catches ~58% of evasive bots, so several sim buttons score high (0.5–0.85) yet sit under the line. Drag the threshold to see the recall / false-positive trade-off — that is the finding, not a bug. Held-out reference at 0.87: 0.9% human FPR, 99.4% naive-bot recall, 58.3% evasive-bot recall (naive AUC 0.9995, evasive AUC 0.9649). Gaps 4–5 — recalibration for a new context, real captured-bot validation — are out of scope here and stay stated as limitations.
